London gets real winters: lake-effect snow, freezing rain and dark evenings by 5 p.m. If this is your first winter behind the wheel, these habits will keep you safe and help you stay calm.

## Get your car ready

- **Winter tires.** They're not required by law in Ontario, but they grip far better on snow and ice than all-season tires. Many insurers also offer a discount for them.
- **Clear the whole car.** Brush snow off the roof, hood, lights and every window, not just a peephole in the windshield. Snow sliding off your roof can blind you or the driver behind you.
- **Top up washer fluid** with a winter mix that won't freeze.
- **Keep an emergency kit** in the car: a brush and scraper, a small shovel, a blanket, a phone charger, water and snacks.
- **Keep your gas tank at least half full** in case you get stuck or delayed.

## Slow down and leave space

Everything takes longer on snow and ice: speeding up, turning and especially **stopping**.

- Drive **below the speed limit** when roads are slippery. The limit is the maximum for good conditions, not a target.
- **Double your following distance** (or more). On ice, it can take many times longer to stop.
- Brake **early and gently**, well before stop signs and intersections.

## Be smooth with every input

Sudden movements are what make a car slide. Accelerate gently, steer smoothly, and brake gradually.

**If your car has ABS** (most modern cars do), press the brake **firmly and hold it** in an emergency. The pedal may pulse or buzz. That's normal, so don't let go.

**If the back of the car starts to slide**, look and steer gently where you **want** to go, and ease off the gas. Don't slam the brakes.

## Watch for black ice

Black ice is a thin, clear layer of ice that looks like a wet road. It forms most often on **bridges, overpasses, shaded spots** and in the early morning. If the road looks wet but there's no spray from other cars' tires, assume it's ice.

## Short days, poor visibility

- Turn on your **full headlights** (not just daytime running lights) whenever visibility is poor, so the back of your car is lit too.
- Keep your windows clear inside and out. Use the defrost before you set off.
- Look further ahead than usual, and watch for pedestrians in dark clothing.

## Practise before you need it

The best place to learn winter driving is an empty parking lot or a quiet road, with an experienced driver beside you. Feel how the car brakes and turns on snow before you face a busy road in a storm.
