The full **G** road test is the last step in Ontario's graduated licensing. It's more advanced than the G2 test, and it includes **highway or expressway driving**. For many students, that's the part that feels scariest. It doesn't have to be.

## When can I take the G test?

You must hold your G2 for at least **12 months** before you can take the G road test. Use that year to build real highway experience, not just a single practice drive the week before.

## Merging onto the highway

Merging is where most nervous drivers struggle. The key is **matching the speed of traffic** before you join it.

1. **Check your mirror** and look over your shoulder early, while you're still on the ramp.
2. **Accelerate firmly** along the ramp. Merging too slowly is more dangerous than merging at the right speed.
3. **Signal**, find your gap, and do a **shoulder check** for your blind spot.
4. **Move in smoothly**, then cancel your signal.

Never stop at the end of a ramp unless traffic leaves you no choice.

## Lane changes

At highway speed, every lane change needs the full routine, every time:

1. **Mirrors:** rear-view, then side mirror.
2. **Signal:** give other drivers time to see it.
3. **Shoulder check:** a quick look over your shoulder into the blind spot.
4. **Move gradually** into the centre of the new lane.
5. **Cancel your signal.**

Don't change lanes in the middle of an interchange, and avoid sitting in another driver's blind spot.

## Managing your speed and space

- Drive **with the flow of traffic**, up to the posted limit. Driving far below the limit is a hazard too.
- Keep **at least a 3-second gap** to the car ahead, and more in rain or snow.
- Use the **right lane** for driving and the left lanes for passing.
- Watch far ahead for brake lights, merging traffic and lane closures, so you can adjust early.

## Exiting safely

1. Know your exit in advance, and move to the right lane early.
2. **Signal** before the exit ramp.
3. Keep your speed up until you're **on the ramp**, then slow down there, not on the highway.
4. Watch for the lower speed limit on the ramp and at the end of it.

## Practise the right way

The best G-test preparation is regular, varied practice: different highways, different times of day, light and heavier traffic. A licensed instructor can take you on the highway safely and give you feedback examiners would.
