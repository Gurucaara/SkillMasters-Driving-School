Welcome to Ontario! If you drove in your home country, you might not have to start from the very beginning. Here's how getting an Ontario licence usually works for newcomers, and where lessons help most.

## How long can I drive on my foreign licence?

New residents can drive in Ontario with a valid licence from another country for a **limited time** after arriving (currently 60 days). After that, you need an Ontario licence. Start the process early: DriveTest appointments can take time to get.

## Option 1: A direct exchange

Ontario has licence-exchange agreements with some countries and provinces. If yours is one of them, and you have enough driving experience, you may be able to exchange your licence for a full Ontario **G** licence without a road test. The list of countries is on [ontario.ca](https://www.ontario.ca/page/exchange-foreign-drivers-licence). Check it first.

## Option 2: Credit for your experience

If your country doesn't have an exchange agreement, your experience can still count. Drivers who can **prove** their past driving experience may be able to skip some of the waiting time in Ontario's graduated licensing system and go straight to a road test.

To get credit you'll usually need:

- Your **foreign driver's licence**, and
- **Official proof of your driving history**, often a letter from the licensing authority in your home country, translated into English or French by an approved translator.

You'll still need to pass the **vision test** and the **G1 knowledge test** on Ontario's rules and signs. DriveTest decides how much credit you get, so bring your documents and ask.

## Why newcomers still take a few lessons

Even confident drivers find a few things different here:

- **Right-of-way rules** at four-way stops and pedestrian crossings.
- **Full stops** at every stop sign, a common reason experienced drivers fail.
- **School buses** with flashing red lights: traffic in **both directions** must stop on roads without a median.
- **Winter driving:** snow, ice and short days.
- **What examiners expect:** exaggerated shoulder checks, steady speed and smooth control.

A few targeted lessons before your road test are often the difference between passing the first time and rebooking.
